Keeping it short today since there are five episodes stacked up waiting for you, so three stories instead of the usual five to seven, picked for how much they should change what you build or what you trust.

Story one. Hugging Face disclosed on Friday that an autonomous agent, not a human operator clicking through an exploit chain, breached its production systems end to end.

A malicious dataset abused two separate code-execution paths at once, a flaw in the platform's remote-code dataset loader and a template-injection bug in how dataset configuration files get parsed, to run code on one of Hugging Face's own processing workers. From there, an agentic system took over completely: it executed tens of thousands of automated actions on its own, escalating privileges and harvesting cloud credentials and internal service tokens without a human re-engaging between steps. Hugging Face says it caught the intrusion through its own LLM-based triage running over security telemetry, separating the real signal from ordinary noise, then revoked and rotated the exposed credentials, patched the underlying loader and template-parsing flaws, and reported the incident to law enforcement. It's still investigating whether any customer or partner data was taken.

Sharp takeaway: this is the same shape as the JadePuffer ransomware story from earlier this month, an agent that plans, executes, and adapts across a long chain with nobody re-prompting it, except this time the target is the repository half the field pulls its models and datasets from, and the defense that caught it was itself an automated triage loop rather than a human analyst. If you load datasets with custom remote code enabled, from Hugging Face or anywhere else, that loader code deserves the same suspicion you already give model weights, and this is a good week to rotate any tokens you've got sitting in a Hugging Face-connected pipeline.

Story two. Google DeepMind scrapped Gemini's existing base architecture entirely and rebuilt Gemini three point five Pro from scratch, rather than extending Gemini two point five Pro incrementally, and shipped it around July seventeenth.

The rebuild targets a two million token context window, doubling Gemini three point one's headline number, plus what Google calls a Deep Think reasoning layer aimed at tasks where getting it right matters more than answering fast: research synthesis, complex code architecture decisions, mathematical proofs, long-horizon planning. Deep Think itself already has a track record to point to, having posted eighty-four point six percent on ARC-AGI-2 and a gold-medal result at the twenty twenty-five International Mathematical Olympiad in its prior incarnation. Google's own blog post is notably light on specifics, though, no confirmed benchmarks, no pricing, no locked-in context number, which means everything above is still one rebuild's worth of claims rather than a verified spec sheet.

Sharp takeaway: choosing a ground-up rebuild over an incremental extension, in the same month DeepSeek shipped a hybrid sparse-attention architecture and Kimi shipped a recurrent-state alternative to the growing key-value cache, tells you Google didn't think its existing attention approach could get to two million tokens cheaply enough by just scaling it up. All three frontier labs are now making a distinct, incompatible architectural bet on how to make long context affordable at the same time, which makes the eventual technical report, once Google actually publishes real numbers instead of a rebuild announcement, worth reading against both of the other two.

Story three. Thinking Machines released Inkling, a nine hundred seventy-five billion parameter open-weights mixture-of-experts model, and its most interesting choice is the thing it left out.

Inkling runs sixty-six decoder-only layers with two hundred fifty-six routed experts plus two shared experts per mixture-of-experts layer, activating only six routed experts per token, which is how a nine-hundred-seventy-five-billion-parameter model runs at a forty-one-billion-active-parameter cost. It handles text, images, and audio natively, with a one million token context window, and was trained on forty-five trillion multimodal tokens. Two choices stand out against how every other frontier model at this scale is built: Inkling drops rotary positional encoding, the near-universal default across Llama, GLM, DeepSeek and nearly everything else, in favor of relative positional embeddings instead, and it skips a separate vision encoder tower entirely, handling image tokens natively within the same architecture rather than gluing an encoder's output into the language model afterward. Attention alternates global and sliding-window layers at a five-to-one ratio with eight key-value heads, which is what keeps that one-million-token context affordable to run. A smaller sibling, Inkling-Small, matches or beats the flagship on several benchmarks with two hundred seventy-six billion total parameters and only twelve billion active.

Sharp takeaway: betting against rotary positional encoding at frontier scale, and against a bolted-on vision encoder, are both genuine architectural departures rather than incremental tuning, the kind worth understanding end to end rather than taking on faith, and that's going into the curriculum as a deep-dive candidate.

Now, what to watch. Watch what Hugging Face's investigation turns up about customer and partner data, since credentials rotated is not the same as damage contained. Watch whether Google backs Gemini three point five Pro's two million token claim with an actual technical report, and how its architecture compares once published against DeepSeek's and Kimi's very different answers to the same long-context problem. Watch Kimi K3's promised July twenty-seventh open-weights release, now six days out, given Moonshot's compute crunch from earlier this week. And keep an eye on NadMesh, the botnet that's been methodically scanning Shodan for exposed Ollama, ComfyUI, and Model Context Protocol servers all month: it's the same exposed-infrastructure story as the Hugging Face breach, just automated at botnet scale instead of one targeted intrusion, and it's worth checking whether anything you've stood up quickly is sitting on the open internet unauthenticated.
